Pitch bands

The ranges the pitch panel counts your voiced time against, each on its own. Bands may overlap, so their shares can add up to more than 100%. Untick Shade to keep a band off the trace while it still counts, and tick the box below to also see the time inside no band.

They are a reference, not a target. Use your clinician's if they differ. Changes apply as you type, and the whole session is recounted against the new bands.

Reference vowels

The diamonds on the vowel plane and the entries in Target. Load a JSON file to use your own: your clinician's values, another language, or your own practice sentences.

It is read here and saved in this browser, never uploaded. The format is on the reference page, beside the two shipped sets to read or download as a starting point. You can also drop the file anywhere on this page.

Pitch

The line is your pitch over the last ten seconds, on a scale that gives each octave the same height. Gaps are silence, or a consonant with no voice in it: the page draws a gap rather than guessing.

The shaded areas are reference bands, which you can change in Settings. The meters underneath show how much of your voiced time this session fell inside each band. Bands can overlap, so the shares can add up to more than 100%. Settings can add a meter for the time inside none of them.

Formants

—

The first three resonances of your vocal tract, over the same ten seconds as the pitch. F1 moves mostly with how open your jaw is, F2 with how far forward your tongue sits, and F3 with the rest of the tract.

These are raw frame-by-frame estimates, so they show the movement between vowels that the vowel plane below deliberately ignores. Gaps are unvoiced.

Brightness

—

The spectral centroid: the balance point of your voice's energy, in Hz. A brighter, more forward sound moves it right; a darker one moves it left. It is what resonance exercises change, and a pitch trace says nothing about it.

There is no target band here, because none is published. Watch which way it moves.

Intonation variability

—

How far your pitch moves, in semitones, over the last ten seconds. The session figure sits beside it. A flat delivery reads as masculine whatever its height.

—semitones

Vocal tract length

—

Estimated from the spacing between F1 and F3, on held vowels only. It shortens when the larynx rises. It is an index for watching change, not an anatomy measurement.

—cm

Vowel plane

Read it as a mouth facing left: up is a higher tongue, left is a fronter one. The green dot is the last vowel you held. It turns solid once the vowel holds still, and stays faint on consonants and transitions.

The diamonds are published averages for women (pink) and men (blue) saying single words. Running speech sits inside them even when nothing about a voice differs, so read position and spread, not distance to a diamond.

Pick a Target to see how far you are from both reference points for that vowel, and which way to move. Neither point is a goal the page chooses for you.

Test vowel: tick it, then click anywhere on the plane to hear a synthetic vowel with its F1 and F2 at that spot, or drag to hear it glide. It plays through the same analysis as a recording, but never into your session: your figures stay as they were.

A click holds for a moment and ends by comparing what was asked with what the analysis heard, in the status line and as a green ring tied to the crosshair. A glide is not compared. Vocal tract sets F3 and above, so the vocal tract length panel's estimate reads it back, and Natural voice adds the small unevenness a real voice has. Where F2 sits below F1 or very near it, no real vowel lives, and the dot may not follow.

What this is

A mirror, not a measurement. Press Start and it shows you your pitch and your vowel as you make them. Each panel's i explains how to read it.

Its numbers are not a measurement of you. The analysis runs on 85 milliseconds of sound at a time, in a browser, through different algorithms from the ones a clinic uses. Read it as a direction, never as a value, and never against a threshold.

Nothing leaves this browser. No upload, no analytics, no account, no server. The microphone is released when you press Stop or close the tab, and every figure on screen dies with it. A take exists only until you download it or leave.

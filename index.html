Pitch bands

The shaded ranges on the pitch trace, and the five zones the share bar counts: below the lower band, the lower band, the gap between, the upper band, above the upper. They are a reference, not a target. Use your clinician's if they differ.

Reference vowels

The diamonds on the vowel plane and the entries in Target. Load a JSON file to use your own: your clinician's values, another language, or your own practice sentences. It is read here and saved in this browser, never uploaded. The format is in data/reference.md.

What this is

A mirror, not a measurement. Press Start and it shows you two things as you make them. The trace is your pitch against reference bands you can set yourself. The plane below it is your vowel, F1 and F2, with published reference vowels as diamonds. Read it as a mouth facing left: up is a higher tongue, left is a fronter one.

Its numbers are not a measurement of you. The analysis runs on 85 milliseconds of sound at a time, in a browser, through different algorithms from the ones a clinic uses. Read it as a direction, never as a value, and never against a threshold.

Running speech sits inside the diamonds even when nothing about a voice differs, because vowels reduce toward the centre when they are not said in isolation. Read position and spread, not distance to a diamond. The reference sets are averages of other people reading isolated words, and neither of them is a target: where they sit is a fact, which of them is a goal is between you and your clinician.

Nothing leaves this browser. No upload, no analytics, no account, no server. The microphone is released when you press Stop or close the tab, and every figure on screen dies with it. A take exists only until you download it or leave.
